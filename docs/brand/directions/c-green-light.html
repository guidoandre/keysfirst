Green Light

The moment that matters is a green light at the door, so that green becomes the brand. The world: signals. Amber means waiting, green means go, the way boarding gates, traffic lights and the landlord's screen work.

Graphics: bold shapes and big numbers

Flat circles and pills, chunky keys and phones, amounts set as graphics. No outlines, no gradients, no glow. Icons use a 2.5 px stroke with rounded ends.

Motion: the green wipe

Fast and crisp (200 to 300 ms), snappy ease-out. When the tenant scans, green floods out from the code across the landlord's screen, once. Reduced-motion settings show it instantly.

Released.Hand over the keys.
  • Enter: 220 ms, 16 px rise. Press: scale to 0.97.
  • Released: circular wipe from the code, 750 ms, then still.

Tone of voice: direct, confident, upbeat

Short lines, verbs first. States the rule like a fact. Celebrates the handover, never the money.

Honest trade-offs

Why it works

  • The most energetic and modern. The "Released" moment becomes the brand's signature, which is great for the demo video.
  • Big and bold, so it's very legible on phones. Amber for locked and green for released is understood without reading.

What to watch

  • The least calm. Bright mint on every screen can feel loud for a €600 decision.
  • Closest to existing green money apps, so it's harder to own.
  • Mint fails as text or as control borders on white (1.7:1). It is a fill colour only.
